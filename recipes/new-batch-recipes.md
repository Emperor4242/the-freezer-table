Each recipe makes 8 portions and supports ½×, 1× and 2× quantities on the website. These are complete written recipes, not kitchen-tested. Photos are AI-generated examples. Timings are estimates; check doneness.

## White bean, barley and root vegetable stew

Prep: 25 min · Cooking: 55–75 min · Makes 8 portions

A hearty one-pot stew of white beans, pearl barley, sweet root vegetables and thyme.

### Ingredients

**Stew**

- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 2 brown onions
- 400 g trimmed leeks
- 500 g carrots
- 400 g parsnips
- 4 garlic cloves
- 250 g dry pearl barley
- 4 × 400 g tins cannellini beans
- 1800 ml vegetable stock
- 2 tsp dried thyme
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 lemon
- 20 g flat-leaf parsley
- salt and black pepper

### Preparation

1. **Prepare the vegetables and beans.** Use an 8-litre stockpot, or split the batch between two pans. Dice the onions; wash and slice the trimmed leeks. Scrub or peel carrots and parsnips and cut into 1.5 cm pieces. Crush the garlic. Rinse the barley. Drain and rinse the beans; four 400 g tins give roughly 960 g drained beans.
2. **Soften the base.** Heat the oil over medium-low heat. Cook onions and leeks for 8–10 minutes, stirring. Add carrots and parsnips for 5 minutes, then garlic and thyme for 1 minute.
3. **Simmer the barley.** Add the barley, stock and bay leaves. Bring to a boil, then partially cover and simmer gently for 30–40 minutes, stirring occasionally. Follow the barley packet if it needs longer. Keep 300–600 ml hot water available and add as needed to prevent sticking.
4. **Add the beans.** Stir in the drained beans and simmer for another 10–15 minutes until the barley and root vegetables are tender. Mash a ladleful of beans and vegetables against the pan to thicken the broth if desired.
5. **Finish and portion.** Remove the bay leaves. Add finely grated lemon zest and 1 tbsp lemon juice, then adjust to taste with more juice, salt and pepper. Stir in chopped parsley. Add water for a spoonable stew rather than a solid mixture. Divide into eight generous bowls or storage portions.

1. **Serve the finished stew.** The preparation steps cook the stew completely. Taste and adjust seasoning, then divide into eight portions. Cool any portions for storage promptly.

### Frozen batch

Defrost in fridge, then reheat on hob · Allow overnight or longer to thaw

1. **Thaw completely.** Defrost a single portion in a covered container in the fridge. Larger containers may need longer than overnight. Do not thaw on the worktop.
2. **Reheat.** Once fully thawed, follow the Defrosted instructions. Reheat only the amount you will eat; do not refreeze reheated leftovers.

### Defrosted

Hob · medium-low heat · About 10–15 min per portion; longer for larger amounts

1. **Loosen and stir.** Put a thawed portion in a saucepan with a splash of water. Heat gently, stirring regularly and adding more water if it catches or looks too thick.
2. **Check the centre.** Bring to a gentle simmer and heat until steaming hot throughout, including any chicken pieces. Check 75°C for at least 30 seconds at the coldest point. Eat within 24 hours of defrosting and reheat only once.

### Storage and notes

Divide into shallow portions and cool promptly; refrigerate or freeze within 1–2 hours. Keep chilled leftovers for no more than 48 hours. Defrost completely in the fridge and use within 24 hours of thawing. Reheat only once until steaming hot throughout; a probe should read 75°C for at least 30 seconds at the coldest point. The barley absorbs liquid during storage, so loosen with water when reheating. Aim to use frozen portions within 3 months for best quality.

The beans, barley and vegetables supply fibre; no fibre-per-portion calculation has been made. Contains barley (gluten); check stock ingredients. Use vegetable stock suitable for your diet. New complete recipe, not kitchen-tested.

## Lemon chicken, butter bean and leek stew

Prep: 25 min · Cooking: 45–55 min · Makes 8 portions

Tender chicken thighs and creamy butter beans in a bright lemon, leek and oregano broth.

### Ingredients

**Stew**

- 1200 g boneless skinless chicken thighs
- 4 × 400 g tins butter beans
- 500 g trimmed leeks
- 400 g carrots
- 1 brown onions
- 4 garlic cloves
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 800 ml vegetable stock
- 2 tsp dried oregano
- 2 bay leaves
- 2 lemons
- 20 g flat-leaf parsley
- salt and black pepper

### Preparation

1. **Prepare.** Use a large heavy pot of at least 6 litres. Wash and slice the leeks, dice the onion, cut carrots into thin half-moons and crush the garlic. Drain and rinse the beans (about 960 g drained). Cut the chicken into roughly 3 cm pieces with a separate board and knife; wash hands and utensils after handling raw chicken.
2. **Brown the chicken.** Heat 1 tbsp oil over medium-high heat. Brown the chicken in two or three batches for 3–4 minutes per batch, then transfer to a bowl. It will still be raw inside at this stage.
3. **Soften the vegetables.** Reduce to medium-low. Add the remaining oil, onion, leeks and carrots. Cook for 8–10 minutes, stirring and adding a splash of water if the base catches. Add garlic and oregano for 1 minute.
4. **Simmer until cooked.** Return the chicken and its juices. Add stock, bay leaves and finely grated zest from one lemon. Bring to a simmer, partly cover and cook gently for about 20 minutes, stirring occasionally. Check the largest chicken pieces reach 75°C for at least 30 seconds; continue cooking if necessary.
5. **Add butter beans.** Add the beans and simmer gently for 8–10 minutes until heated through and the carrots are tender. Crush a few beans into the broth for a naturally creamy texture. Add a little water if too thick.
6. **Finish with lemon.** Remove the bay leaves. Stir in 2 tbsp lemon juice and chopped parsley, then season. Taste before adding more juice. Reserve the second lemon for wedges or to brighten reheated portions. Divide into eight portions; serve alone or with bread if wanted (bread is not included in the shopping list).

## Broccoli, pea and white bean macaroni cheese

Prep: 30 min · Cooking: 25–35 min bake · Makes 8 portions

Wholewheat macaroni, broccoli, peas and white beans in a generous Cheddar and mustard sauce.

### Ingredients

**Pasta and vegetables**

- 500 g dry wholewheat macaroni
- 600 g broccoli
- 300 g frozen peas
- 2 × 400 g tins cannellini beans

**Sauce and topping**

- 60 g butter
- 60 g plain flour
- 1000 ml milk
- 300 ml vegetable stock
- 300 g mature Cheddar
- 2 tsp Dijon mustard
- 60 g wholemeal breadcrumbs
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- salt and black pepper

### Preparation

1. **Prepare the dishes.** If baking now, heat the oven to 180°C fan. Use two shallow 2.5-litre oven dishes or several smaller dishes with at least 5 litres total capacity, leaving headroom. Cut broccoli into small florets and dice the tender stalk. Drain and rinse the beans (about 480 g drained). Grate the cheese.
2. **Cook pasta and vegetables.** Cook the macaroni in a large pan of boiling water for 2 minutes less than the packet time. Add broccoli for the final 3 minutes and peas for the final minute. If the pasta cooks too quickly, blanch the vegetables in a second pan. Drain well; the pasta should still be slightly firm.
3. **Make the sauce.** Melt the butter in a large saucepan over medium-low heat. Stir in flour and cook for 2 minutes. Gradually whisk in milk, followed by stock, keeping the sauce smooth. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook for 5–7 minutes, stirring, until thickened but still pourable.
4. **Add cheese and beans.** Take the pan off the heat. Stir in 200 g of the Cheddar and the mustard, then add the drained beans. Season lightly, as cheese and stock may already be salty. The sauce should generously coat the pasta; loosen with a splash of water if necessary.
5. **Assemble.** Mix pasta, vegetables and sauce, then divide between the dishes. For immediate baking, top with the remaining 100 g cheese and the breadcrumbs mixed with olive oil. Follow the Fresh tab to bake. For freezing, cool the assembled mixture promptly without baking; pack the cheese and dry breadcrumbs separately, and add oil and topping when ready to bake.

### Fresh

180°C fan · 25–35 min

1. **Bake once.** Bake the freshly assembled, warm pasta dishes for 25–35 minutes until bubbling and golden. Check the centre is steaming hot; if the topping browns too quickly, cover loosely with foil. Allow longer for a chilled or deeper dish.
2. **Serve.** Rest for 5 minutes, then divide into eight portions. The preparation steps do not include this bake; do not bake twice.

### Frozen batch

Defrost in fridge, then bake · Allow 24–36 hours for a larger dish

1. **Thaw first.** Defrost the covered, assembled pasta completely in the fridge. Small portions may thaw overnight; larger dishes can take 24–36 hours or longer. This recipe does not rely on putting frozen cookware into a hot oven.
2. **Bake after thawing.** Once fully thawed, add the reserved cheese and breadcrumbs mixed with oil, then follow the Defrosted tab. Use within 24 hours of complete thawing.

### Defrosted

180°C fan · 35–50 min for shallow dishes

1. **Prepare.** Use oven-safe cookware according to its instructions. If the pasta has absorbed the sauce, gently work a small splash of water into the edges. Add the reserved topping if the pasta was frozen before baking.
2. **Heat through.** Cover loosely with foil and bake for about 25–35 minutes. Uncover for another 10–15 minutes to brown. Check the centre is steaming hot and reaches 75°C for at least 30 seconds; allow longer for deeper dishes. Reheat only once.

### Storage and notes

For best texture, freeze assembled portions before the final bake, keeping the cheese and dry breadcrumbs separately wrapped; label them with the pasta. Cool quickly in shallow portions and refrigerate or freeze within 1–2 hours. Aim to use within 2–3 months for quality. Defrost fully in the fridge, use within 24 hours, then bake as directed. Already-baked leftovers can also be frozen promptly and reheated once; do not add another full topping.

Wholewheat pasta, white beans, broccoli and peas provide fibre. Choose vegetarian Cheddar if needed; contains wheat/gluten, milk and mustard. Keep the sauce loose because pasta absorbs it during storage. New complete recipe, not kitchen-tested.
